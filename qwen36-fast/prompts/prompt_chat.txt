Assistant: For a 6-year-old word-a-day app with weekly quiz, skip the PWA. Audio playback reliability on locked Android is iffy, and you will want offline TTS which is painful from a service worker. Flutter is the best answer for this specific scope. Reasons: built-in just_audio and flutter_tts cover the pronounce-and-hear use case, Hive or Drift gives you a flat-file DB for progress and the five-word-per-week buffer, and the UI toolkit lets you do big tappable cards and simple animations without fighting the framework. Native Kotlin is overkill for a single-device personal app you will maintain for five minutes a month. React Native you would end up glueing two audio libraries and a notification library with worse documentation than Flutter equivalents. A week of evenings gets you something shipped on your daughter's tablet. For the daily-word content, do not hand-curate. Use a 200-word seed list from a published CEFR A1 or Dolch list, randomize presentation order, and log which words she got right in the Friday quiz so you can surface the misses again two weeks later. Built-in spaced repetition without the Anki gloss.

Assistant: Generate weekly content packs on the homelab, rsync-style to a cheap object store the Android app pulls from on Wi-Fi. Flow: Sunday evening she picks a topic in the app, it writes the choice to a local SQLite row, next time it sees Wi-Fi it POSTs the choice to a small FastAPI endpoint you run behind a Tailscale subnet or a Cloudflare Tunnel.
